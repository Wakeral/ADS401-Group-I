# ADS401-Group-I

**Research Question**
How does AI-assisted literature reading and summarization influence students' independent comprehension of academic papers? 

**Research Context**
In an era of AI, AI has integrated itself in students' lives educationally and recreationally. There is no avoiding students using AI for study help, research assistance, or literature review. However, it remains important to understand how AI assistance influences students' confidence in interpreting research papers and their actual comprehension of academic content.

This study focuses on XJTLU Master's and PhD students, exploring how they integrate AI into reading and understanding academic papers relevant to their thesis projects. Through a survey and task-based evaluation, the study examines students' AI usage practices and evaluates how AI-generated explanations and summaries shape their confidence, perceived accuracy, and understanding of research content. 

**What We Did**
The first round of data collection, we collected survey data on seven distinct parts:
  1. Part A (Demographics): Collects basic background information, including student status, level of study, and departmental affiliation.
  2. Part B (Background & Language): Establishes a baseline for the student's thesis progress, prior experience with academic reading, and language proficiency.
  3. Part C (AI Literacy): Measures the student's self-reported ability to use AI tools effectively, understand their limitations, and navigate university policies.
  4. Part D (AI in Reading & Writing): Explores the specific workflows students employ when using AI, including the tasks they delegate to AI, their prompting language, and their verification habits.
  5. Part E (Academic Integrity Anxiety): Assesses the level of uncertainty or anxiety students feel regarding the boundaries between acceptable AI assistance and academic misconduct.
  6. Part F (Perceived Effects on Understanding): Captures students' subjective beliefs about whether AI assistance has strengthened or weakened their independent comprehension skills over time.
  7. Part G (Discipline, Language & Context): Investigates how AI reliance might vary based on the technical difficulty of the paper, the relevance to their thesis, and whether they are reading in their first or second language.

With the data collected from the survey, we conducted a task evaluation.
  1. Pre-Task Questionnaire
      Participants complete an initial questionnaire collecting demographic information, AI usage frequency, AI proficiency, and general trust in AI tools.
  2. Task 1: Research Paper Comprehension
      Participants use an AI tool to identify five key elements of the given research paper:
        - Research question
        - Methodology
        - Independent variable(s)
        - Dependent variable(s)
        - Limitations
      After reviewing the AI-generated answers, participants rate their confidence, perceived accuracy, and clarity. They then read the original paper and review their answers against its content before providing a second set of ratings.
  3. Task 2: AI-Assisted Paper Summarization
      Participants use AI to generate a summary of the same research paper. They initially evaluate the AI-generated summary by rating their confidence, perceived accuracy, and clarity. They then read the original paper, review the summary, and provide a second set of ratings.
  4. Post-Task Questionnaire
      Following both tasks, participants complete a final questionnaire assessing their confidence, trust in AI-generated content, perceived accuracy, and difficulty when using AI for academic literature reading.
